Hot water at the time each person needs it.

Everyone in the house sets their shower times. Hot Water Ready works out when to switch the heater on from the water's temperature and how fast your tank really heats, keeps it hot while it's needed, and tells each person on their phone when it's ready. The rest of the day the heater stays off.

    How it decides

    1

    Everyone sets their times

    Each person adds ready times on a clock face, like a phone alarm, and the days they repeat. Times close together share one heating window, so the heater keeps going between two showers instead of letting the water cool.

    2

    It starts as late as it can

    The start is the degrees still to go, times the minutes your heater needs per degree, plus a margin. That rate is learned from the heater's own clean heats, so a fast tank starts late and a slow one early. Colder water starts earlier, warmer water later.

    3

    Hot, then quiet

    At the target it tells the people it heated for, and holds the water in a band around the target until their window ends. Then the heater stays off until the next one.

    Heat now

    Hot water outside the plan. Several people can ask; each is told when it's ready. Hold the button to keep it hot for 5 minutes to 2 hours.

    Your own temperature

    Administrators can heat one request to anything from 30 to 45 °C without touching the heater's target. The highest request wins.

    Private times

    A member can keep their times to themselves. Others' cards leave them out; the administrators still see them.

    Switch off for everyone

    Administrators can stop a heat, with a confirmation that names who loses out. Those people are told.

    Faults, said plainly

    A silent sensor, a heater that doesn't warm the water, a relay stuck on: a red banner, a Repairs entry and a message.

    Away

    Away pauses a person's times, until a last day if you like, and ends by itself.

    Two probes

    A second probe lower in the tank makes the start allow for cold water below the top, and notices a probe coming loose.

    Compact card

    For a dashboard the whole house sees: the ring, the status and the sentence under it, no buttons. Each person sees only what the full card would show them; a tap can open the full card.

    Install

    1. Add the repository to HACS. In Home Assistant's Marketplace, add coin/hot-water-ready as a custom repository of type Integration.
    2. Install and restart. Install Hot Water Ready, then restart Home Assistant.
    3. Add the heater. Settings → Devices & services → Add integration. Choose the heater (a switch or a thermostat), the water temperature sensor and, if you have one, a power sensor.
    4. Add the people. On the heater, Add member: a Home Assistant person, or a virtual one such as Guests.
    5. Put the card on a dashboard. The integration loads it itself; there is no resource to add. For a shared dashboard, the compact card shows only the top, with no buttons.
    card.yaml
    type: custom:hot-water-ready-card
# entry_id: optional, the heater to show
# when the house has more than one

# Compact, for a shared dashboard:
# compact: true
# tap_action:  # optional, else a tap does nothing
#   action: navigate
#   navigation_path: /dashboard-home/showers

    What you need

    • Home Assistant 2026.10 or later.
    • Something that heats the water: a switch on the element, or a thermostat (climate entity) with the modes heat and off.
    • A temperature sensor in the water.
    • Optional: a power sensor on the heater, to notice a heater that draws nothing or a relay stuck on.
    • Optional: a second probe lower in the tank.

    From automations and voice

    The actions follow the same permissions as the card. Members are named by their device.

    Heat now, hotter, kept an hour
    action: hot_water_ready.heat_now
data:
  heater: <config entry id>
  member: <device id of a member>  # who is told
  target: 44         # optional, 30 to 45 °C
  keep_minutes: 60   # optional
    Add a ready time
    action: hot_water_ready.add_ready_time
data:
  member: <device id of a member>
  time: "07:00"
  weekdays: [mon, tue, wed, thu, fri]
    Away until after Christmas
    action: hot_water_ready.set_away
data:
  member: <device id of a member>
  away: true
  until: "2026-12-26"
    Switch off for everyone
    action: hot_water_ready.switch_off
data:
  heater: <config entry id>

    Hot Water Ready is open source, on GitHub, where the README explains every option. The demo above simplifies the integration's controller to run in your browser; your own house runs the real one.
